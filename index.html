Our Ise-Shima Tours

All of our Ise-Shima tours are private, small-group and guided in English. Looking for the best things to do in Ise-Shima? Ise Jingu Tour

A private guided tour of Ise Jingu's Inner Shrine (Naiku) — Japan's most sacred Shinto site.
Learn proper worship etiquette, experience purification rituals, and discover stories you won't find in any guidebook.
Departs at 9:00 or 14:00. Guided portion at Naiku is approximately 2 hours.

　

About this tour

Ise Jingu is Japan's holiest Shinto shrine — yet most visitors spend only an hour and leave without understanding what they have seen.

This tour focuses on the Inner Shrine (Naiku), home to Amaterasu, the sun goddess at the heart of the Shinto faith. With a knowledgeable local guide, you will move through the ancient forested approach at the right pace, gaining a deeper understanding of the worship rituals and spiritual significance that define this sacred place.

If you wish, you can also add an optional visit to the Outer Shrine (Geku) with car transportation (¥5,000 per person). When selected, the tour follows the traditional order: Geku → Naiku, adding approximately 1.5 hours to the experience.

After the guided tour, Okage Yokocho and Oharai-machi — the historic merchant streets just a short walk from Naiku — are yours to explore independently at your own pace.

Optional: visit to the Outer Shrine (Geku) with car transportation (+approx. 1.5 hours · ¥5,000 / per person).

Pearl Experience in Ise-Shima

Harvest a real akoya pearl at a working pearl farm on Ago Bay — where cultured pearls began

Ise-Shima is where the cultured pearl began. A narrow mountain road — just wide enough for a small Japanese car — leads down to a working pearl farm on Ago Bay, where akoya pearls are grown and made into jewellery on the same site.
This is not a visitor attraction. It is a working farm, and you take part in what happens there.
Two experiences are available. Choose the one you prefer below. Maximum 5 guests.

　

About this experience

The world's first cultured pearl was grown here in Ise-Shima, and Ago Bay has been the centre of akoya pearl farming ever since. More than a century later, small family farms still work their own rafts across the bay.

One of them takes guests. You drive in along a steep, narrow mountain road, arrive at the workshop, and from there you are not watching pearl farming — you are doing it, alongside the people who do it every day.

Sightseeing shows you the surface of a place. This takes you into the work that the region is actually built on.

　

Choose your experience

1 · Pearl Harvesting

1–2 hours  ·  Maximum 5 guests

Choose an oyster from the farm's own stock and open it with your own hands.

At the workshop you are shown the farm's cultured oysters and choose one yourself. Then you open the shell and lift out the pearl with your own hands.

Every pearl is different in shape, colour and size, and nobody — not even the farmer — knows what is inside until the shell is open.

Afterwards there is time to feed the fish kept beside the rafts, and back at the shop you can have your pearl set into jewellery if you would like to.

The pearls, moments after the shell was opened.

2 · Nucleus Insertion & Harvesting

3–4 hours  ·  6 oysters per person  ·  Maximum 5 guests

Learn the craft itself — place the nucleus into six oysters, then return them to the sea.

Nucleation is the technique at the heart of akoya pearl farming, and it has been handed down in Ago Bay for generations. Here a craftsman who does it for a living shows you how — and then you do it yourself.

Each guest works on six oysters, placing a nucleus and a small piece of mantle tissue inside each one. It is close, delicate work that asks for a steady hand and real concentration.

The oysters you have worked on go into nets and back into the sea. About a year later, any pearls that can be harvested from your own shells are collected and sent to your home address.

You also choose an oyster from the farm's stock and harvest a pearl on the day — so you leave with a pearl in your hand, and another one growing in the bay.

Learning the craft from a craftsman, returning your own shells to the sea, and finding out a year later what grew inside them. This is as close to Ise-Shima pearl farming as a visitor can get.

Not every oyster survives the year. Where pearls can be harvested from your shells, all of them are sent to you — but in some cases none of the shells survive and no pearl can be collected. The odds vary considerably with the season.

This is technical work and may be difficult for younger children.

Goma Fire Ritual in Ise-Shima

A Shingon fire ceremony to burn away misfortune, at a temple founded in 742

Take part in goma — the sacred fire ritual of esoteric Shingon Buddhism — inside a temple founded in 742, in Ise-Shima National Park.
Monks chant sutras while gomagi prayer sticks burn: a yakuyoke ceremony to ward off bad luck and purify your wishes.
A morning of roughly 2.5 hours. Departs at 9:00. The tour typically ends around 11:30–12:00, though this may vary depending on travel time. For guests aged 15 and over.
Held indoors in the temple hall, the ritual goes ahead rain or shine — a good choice for a rainy day in Shima.

　

About this experience

This experience was created for travellers who want two things at once: the nature of Ise-Shima National Park, and Japanese culture as it is actually practised — not performed for visitors.

The temple was founded in 742, when Emperor Shomu prayed here for the safe construction of Todai-ji in Nara. According to temple tradition, Kukai — the founder of Shingon, Japan's esoteric Buddhist school — established its teachings here in 807, and the temple has kept them ever since.

At the heart of your visit is the goma ritual. Wooden prayer sticks are burned in a sacred hearth, and the rising flames carry prayers to Fudo Myoo, the fierce guardian of Shingon Buddhism. The fire is believed to burn away worldly desires and misfortune, purifying each wish before it reaches him.

You sit close to the fire, in the clear air of Ise-Shima, as the sutras are chanted. It is a quiet, direct encounter with Japan's spiritual culture — and something very few visitors ever see.

About Me

Inaka Tours began with a simple comment from someone I deeply respect: "I wish more people would come to this region."

That moment made me realize something important. This area is special, but there was no way for visitors to truly experience it. So I decided to study English and moved to Australia for two years.

Living overseas gave me many new experiences and helped me learn about different cultures — but it also made me appreciate Japan even more.

When I returned to Japan in October 2025, I decided to start Inaka Tours here.

This region doesn't have a long list of famous attractions. But I believe that's exactly the beauty of rural Japan.

As I explored this region more deeply, I realized its greatest charm isn't places — it's what we call "Shima time," a slow and relaxed atmosphere.

With Inaka Tours, my goal is to share it with visitors who want to experience the real Japan.

Leave everyday life behind and embrace "Shima time" in the beautiful Ise-Shima region — an unforgettable retreat experience during your trip to Japan.

I look forward to meeting you and sharing this special experience with you.
