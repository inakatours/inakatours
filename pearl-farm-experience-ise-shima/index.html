The cultured pearl was invented here. Fully private, guided in English, with a car from your hotel door.

Not a museum

A working farm, not a visitor attraction

Ise-Shima has plenty of places to look at pearls. There are museums, exhibition halls and shops with a demonstration counter, and they are well made. This is not one of them.

This is a small family farm that grows akoya pearls on rafts in Ago Bay and makes jewellery from them on the same site. Nothing has been built for visitors. There is no exhibition, no set route and no gift-shop circuit — there is a workshop, a bench, trays of oysters, and the people who do this for a living.

You are not watching pearl farming. For a couple of hours you are taking part in it, next to the person whose work it is.

That is the part travellers remember, and it is the part that cannot be arranged at a counter.

Why private matters here

Only your group — and a car from your door

Every booking is fully private. You are never joined to another group, and the pace follows you: if you want to spend twenty minutes choosing an oyster, or stay at the bench asking questions, nothing is running on a timetable behind you.

This matters more than it sounds, because the farm is small. Two or three guests at a bench is a conversation; a coach party is a queue.

It also makes the journey possible. The farm sits at the end of a steep, narrow road with no bus service and nowhere obvious to park. Without a car you simply cannot get there. We collect you from your hotel, drive you in, and bring you back — the drive itself, winding down through the trees to the water, is part of the day.

Choose your experience

Two ways to spend the morning

1 · Pearl Harvesting

1–2 hours  ·  Fully private  ·  Hotel pickup included

At the workshop you are shown the farm's cultured oysters and choose one yourself. Then you open the shell and lift out the pearl with your own hands.

Every pearl is different in shape, colour and size, and nobody — not even the farmer — knows what is inside until the shell is open. Some come out perfectly round; others are baroque, with their own irregular shape. That is the nature of it, and part of why it is worth doing.

Afterwards there is time to feed the fish kept beside the rafts, and back at the shop you can have your pearl set into jewellery if you would like to.

  • Pickup at your hotel by private car
  • Drive up to the farm along the mountain road
  • Choose your oyster at the workshop
  • Open the shell and take out your pearl
  • Feed the fish
  • Back at the shop — optional jewellery setting
  • Drop-off

Good for: first-time visitors, families with children, and anyone with half a morning who wants something they could not have done anywhere else.

2 · Nucleus Insertion & Harvesting

3–4 hours  ·  6 oysters each  ·  Fully private  ·  Hotel pickup included

Nucleation is the technique at the heart of akoya pearl farming, and it has been handed down in Ago Bay for generations. Here a craftsman who does it for a living shows you how — and then you do it yourself.

Each guest works on six oysters, placing a nucleus and a small piece of mantle tissue inside each one. It is close, delicate work that asks for a steady hand and real concentration.

The oysters you have worked on go into nets and back into the sea. About a year later, any pearls that can be harvested from your own shells are collected and sent to your home address. You also choose an oyster from the farm's stock and harvest a pearl on the day — so you leave with a pearl in your hand, and another one growing in the bay.

  • Pickup at your hotel by private car
  • Explanation and preparation at the workshop
  • Demonstration by a professional
  • You insert the nucleus and the mantle tissue — six oysters each
  • Your oysters go into nets and back into the sea
  • Choose an oyster to harvest, open the shell and take out your pearl
  • Feed the fish
  • Back at the shop — optional jewellery setting
  • Drop-off

Good for: travellers who want to understand how a pearl is actually made, and who like the idea of something of theirs still growing in Ago Bay a year after the trip ends.

Getting here, and what to expect

Pickup and travel times

Hotel pickup and drop-off are included for guests staying in the Ise, Toba and Shima areas. If you are staying elsewhere, we meet you at Ugata Station.

Questions

Frequently asked

Is this a real pearl farm or a museum?

It is a real, family-run akoya pearl farm on Ago Bay. The people who show you the work are the people who do it for a living, and the oysters come from the farm's own rafts. There is no exhibition hall and no gift-shop circuit — you are in the workshop where the pearls are actually produced.

Is the experience private?

Yes. Every booking is fully private — only your own group, up to five guests. You are not joined to other travellers, and the pace of the visit follows you.

Is hotel pickup included?

Yes. Pickup and drop-off by private car are included for guests staying in the Ise, Toba and Shima areas. If you are staying elsewhere, we can collect you from Ugata Station, which is reached from Osaka in about 2.5 hours by limited express train with no transfers. The farm is on a narrow mountain road with no bus service, so transport is part of the experience rather than an extra.

Do I keep the pearl I harvest?

Yes. The pearl you take out of the shell on the day is yours to keep. These are real akoya pearls, so some come out perfectly round and others are baroque, with their own irregular shape — nobody knows what is inside until the shell is opened.

What is the difference between the two experiences?

Pearl Harvesting takes one to two hours: you choose an oyster from the farm's stock and open it yourself. Nucleus Insertion and Harvesting takes three to four hours: a craftsman teaches you to place a nucleus and mantle tissue into six oysters, which then go back into the sea for about a year, and you also harvest a pearl on the day.

What happens to the oysters I work on in the nucleation experience?

They go into nets and back into Ago Bay for about a year. Any pearls that can be harvested from your own shells are collected and sent to your home address. Not every oyster survives the year, and in some cases no pearl can be collected — the odds vary considerably with the season.

Can children take part?

Children are welcome at the harvesting experience and usually enjoy it a great deal. The nucleation experience is close, delicate work with fine tools and may be difficult for younger children.

Can I have my pearl made into jewellery?

Often, yes. The same site makes jewellery, so a pearl can be set into a pendant or a ring at the workshop. This depends on the workshop's schedule on the day, so we check it for you with every booking.

How much does it cost?

The price depends on which experience you choose and how many guests are coming, because private car transport and the guide are shared across the group. Send us your dates and group size and we will come back to you with the price, usually the same day.

How far in advance should I book?

As early as you can. This is a small working farm and every booking has to be confirmed with them before it is final, so a few days' notice makes a real difference — especially in spring and autumn.

What if it rains?

Most of the experience takes place inside the workshop, so rain is rarely a problem. Rough weather at sea can occasionally affect the farm's work, in which case we will contact you and arrange another date.

Can this be combined with Ise Jingu or other sightseeing?

Yes. Because you have a car and a guide for the day, the pearl farm pairs naturally with Ise Jingu, the Ago Bay coastline or a local lunch. Tell us what else you would like to see and we will plan the day around it.

Ask about a date

This is a small working farm, so every booking is confirmed with them before it is final. Send your dates, the number of guests and which of the two experiences you would like — we will come back to you with availability and the price, usually the same day.
